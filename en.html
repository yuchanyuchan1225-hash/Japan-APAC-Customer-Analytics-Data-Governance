01 / Business problem

The source can be reportable without producing a comparable customer metric.

01

Line items and orders are mixed

An order with several product lines can be double counted when order value is summed at line-item level.

02

Identity breaks across journeys

Guest checkout, account creation, and email variation can turn one person into several analytical customers.

03

Operational exceptions distort signals

Cancellations, internal use, benefits, and delivery rules should not be interpreted as ordinary customer behavior.

02 / Analytical definitions

F1 and F2 are definitions, not labels.

F1 means the first completed order within the observation window. F2 means a later completed order from the same resolved customer. Neither measure claims to identify a customer's first-ever purchase.

First purchase Call it “new” without full history Name it first purchase within the observation window
F1 / F2 customer counts Add overlapping customers together Recalculate total customers with a unique identity key

03 / Data governance

A controlled path from source data to an interpretable result.

01 Inspect source data Confirm grain, statuses, required fields, and the observation period.
02 Normalize to order level Use a representative order record for order value and status.
03 Resolve identity Connect account and guest evidence into a documented analytical key.
04 Assign lifecycle stage Sequence completed orders and assign F1 or F2.
05 Reconcile and explain Test totals and retain exceptions, limits, and naming rules.
04 / Synthetic example

A comparison designed to prevent the wrong conclusion.

Synthetic illustration
If a month contains 120 F1 customers and 18 F2 customers, but six people placed both their first and repeat orders in that month, the unique-customer count is 132, not 138. The correct denominator depends on the question being asked.

Reporting discipline

“Revenue from repeat customers” and “F2-order revenue” are different measures. The former may include those customers’ first orders or cancelled orders. The metric name must match its calculation.

06 / Operational value

Higher-quality analysis supports the next operational decision.

CRM audience design

A stable customer key makes eligibility for member and repeat-purchase activity more consistent.

Exception management

Separating unusual transactions from normal demand prevents operational rules from becoming false customer insights.

Explainable reporting

Definitions, limits, and reconciliation checks let future teams reproduce the same interpretation.
